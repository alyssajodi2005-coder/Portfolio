01

The overview

A little context.

KneeGuard explores how a wearable knee-sleeve concept, Arduino-based sensor integration, and a digital interface can support ACL rehabilitation information. It is a prototype, with a linked demo.

Explore the live demo
02

The challenge

The problem worth solving.

Recovery-related information can be difficult to interpret. The design opportunity is to make information understandable for people navigating ACL rehabilitation, while being clear about the limits of a prototype. Patient and clinician needs have not been validated here.

Design goals & considerations

  1. Present recovery information with clear hierarchy and accessible language.
  2. Connect the wearable concept, sensor inputs, and digital interface.
  3. Keep prototype information distinct from medical advice or clinical claims.

03 / THE PROCESS

01 · People & context

People recovering from ACL injuries are the intended audience. Research findings, interviews, and clinical requirements have not been supplied; these remain open questions.

02 · Hardware + software

The project combines a wearable knee-sleeve concept with Arduino-based sensor integration and a digital recovery interface. A component diagram, sensor specifications, and implementation details are pending.

03 · Information & interface

Clarity, accessibility, and thoughtful information presentation are central design considerations. The live interface groups current knee flexion, today’s recovery milestone, and progress status into three distinct cards. Week and day selectors establish the recovery context, with start/stop controls for a live read. This describes the visible prototype, not validated sensor behavior.

04 · Prototype & next steps

The live demo is linked below. Future exploration could include clearer explanations of sensor data and accessibility evaluation. These are potential improvements, not completed testing.
